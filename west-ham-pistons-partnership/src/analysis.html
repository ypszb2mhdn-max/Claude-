A strategic partnership proposal between West Ham United FC and the Detroit Pistons (NBA)
“Two works teams. Two working-class cities. Two comebacks.”

Executive summary

West Ham United starts the 2026/27 season in the EFL Championship after relegation in May 2026 ended 14 years in the Premier League. Its six existing partnerships are all commercial (kit, technology & services, finance, fan & lifestyle, global partners & operations, communications). None involves another sports club, and none transfers sporting or organisational knowledge.

We propose a three-year strategic partnership with the Detroit Pistons. Both clubs began as the works team of an industrial company, both represent working-class cities that declined and are regenerating, and the Pistons have just completed the turnaround West Ham now needs: from 14 wins and 68 losses in 2023/24 to first place in the NBA Eastern Conference (60-22) in 2025/26.

This is a partnership, not a sponsorship: no rights fee is paid in either direction, both clubs contribute resources the other lacks, and the partnership is run jointly. The primary objective is fan experience and engagement, supported by two further pillars: performance and turnaround knowledge exchange, and community impact in Newham and Detroit.

The Pistons were selected from a long list of 11 clubs through a two-stage screening. They score highest or joint-highest on every one of the six selection criteria (4.70 out of 5), so the choice does not depend on how the criteria are weighted.

1Strategic context: what West Ham needs

1.1 Where the club stands (verified facts)

  • Industrial origins. The club was founded in 1895 as Thames Ironworks FC, the works team of the Thames Ironworks and Shipbuilding Company, and became West Ham United in 1900. The crossed hammers on the badge are riveting hammers from the shipyard.
  • Stadium. In 2016 the club moved from the Boleyn Ground to the London Stadium (about 62,500 seats), where it is a tenant rather than the owner.
  • Recent high and low. West Ham won the UEFA Europa Conference League in 2023. Three years later it finished 18th in the 2025/26 Premier League with 39 points and was relegated on the final day.
  • Current situation. In 2026/27 the club plays in the Championship (46 league games instead of 38). A change of head coach was reported in early November 2026.

1.2 How the club describes itself

In its 2026 report (as presented in the lecture), West Ham describes itself as strong, resilient and sustainable, with a balanced mix of older and younger players, strong pride in its community, a commitment to investing in fan experience, and an ambition to grow at home and abroad. Our interpretation: relegation contradicts “strong” on the pitch, but it makes the other values (resilience, community, fan experience) more important, not less. A partnership should help the club live up to these values during a difficult period.

1.4 Strategic needs (our interpretation)

  1. N1 – Keep and deepen fan engagement. Relegation reduces broadcast exposure and risks supporters drifting away.
  2. N2 – Learn how to rebuild. The club needs know-how on organisational turnaround: leadership, culture, recruitment and decision-making.
  3. N3 – Improve player availability. A 46-game Championship season puts more load on the squad.
  4. N4 – Protect the international profile. In particular, grow in the US, where interest in football is high after the 2026 World Cup in North America.
  5. N5 – Strengthen community impact in Newham, the club’s home borough.
One primary goalThe lecture warned that one partner cannot cover all five sectors. We therefore set N1, fan experience and engagement, as the primary goal. It was also the top motive for partnerships in the research presented in the lecture. N2, N3 and N5 are supporting pillars, and N4 is an expected outcome.

3The partner: Detroit Pistons

3.1 Profile

  • A works team, like West Ham. The club was founded in 1941 in Fort Wayne, Indiana, as the Fort Wayne Zollner Pistons, the company team of Fred Zollner’s piston-manufacturing business. It moved to Detroit in 1957.
  • Identity. Three NBA championships (1989, 1990, 2004) and the “Bad Boys” reputation for toughness have given the club a gritty, blue-collar identity in the Motor City.
  • A club that also moved home. The Pistons returned to downtown Detroit in 2017 (Little Caesars Arena) and opened a performance centre in Detroit in 2019. Like West Ham, they had to build a matchday identity in a new home.
  • The downfall. In 2023/24 the Pistons won 14 games and lost 68, including a record 28 consecutive defeats.
  • The turnaround. In 2024 a new president of basketball operations and a new head coach were appointed. The team made the play-offs in 2024/25 and then finished 60-22, first in the Eastern Conference, in 2025/26, winning its first play-off series since 2008.
  • The city. Detroit went through the largest municipal bankruptcy in US history in 2013 and is now regenerating. This mirrors East London, where the docks closed and the area was later rebuilt around the 2012 Olympic Games.

The partnership combines both types presented in the lecture. It is a similarity partnership in identity, like 1. FC Köln and the Detroit Lions, and a complementary partnership in resources and markets, like Bayern Munich and FC Dallas. Because the clubs play different sports on different continents, there is no direct competition.

3.3 Type of relationship

Using the lecture’s distinction, this is a strategic partnership, not a sponsorship. There is no exchange of money for logo rights: both clubs combine resources and both create value. If the year-one evaluation is positive, the partnership is designed to deepen into a strategic alliance with long-term commitments on both sides.

4.1 Three pillars

Pillar 1 · Primary

“Comeback Cities” fan experience

  • A Pistons Day at the London Stadium and a West Ham Night at Little Caesars Arena, once per season each
  • Co-produced documentary series “Works Teams” about the two clubs’ heritage and comebacks
  • Watch parties in each city; ticket packages for official supporters’ clubs
  • Pistons’ game-presentation team advises on London Stadium atmosphere
Pillar 2

“Rebuild Lab” performance & knowledge

  • Two staff exchanges per year (performance, medical, analytics), timed for each club’s off-season
  • Shared methods on load management, injury prevention, recovery and sleep, using anonymised data only
  • Leadership sessions on how the Pistons reset culture, recruitment and decision-making
  • West Ham shares academy-pathway design with the Pistons’ development (G League) programme
Pillar 3

“Two Cities Rising” community

  • The West Ham United Foundation and the Pistons’ community team run one joint youth programme on sport participation and employability
  • Annual youth exchange: 10 young people each way between Newham and Detroit
  • Shared impact measurement and a joint annual impact report

Commercial outcomes follow from the pillars. We do not treat them as a separate goal. They include a co-branded “Works Collection” merchandise capsule, joint content that both clubs can offer to their commercial partners (subject to existing exclusivity agreements), and new fans in each other’s markets.

4.2 Why together is better than alone

  • Neither club can buy what the other has. The Pistons cannot build credibility in a London community from Detroit, and West Ham cannot hire an organisation that has just completed an elite turnaround. This is the core argument of resource-dependence theory.
  • Lower cost and risk. Each club can test new matchday and performance ideas from the other before investing its own money, a point the lecture made about facilities and resources.
  • A shared story is stronger than two separate ones. “Two works teams, two cities, two comebacks” is a narrative neither club can tell alone. Fans are more likely to trust a new club when their own club introduces it, which the lecture described as the trust transfer effect.

6Governance and implementation

6.1 Structure and responsibilities

  • Approval. A memorandum of understanding (MoU) is signed by both clubs’ chief executives and approved by each club’s board and ownership.
  • Joint Steering Group. Three senior representatives per club (commercial, sporting operations, community) meet quarterly online, with an annual summit that alternates between London and Detroit.
  • Partnership managers. One named lead per club runs the partnership day to day and keeps a value ledger: a record of what each side contributes and receives, reviewed quarterly to prevent a power imbalance.
  • Pillar leads.
    • Fan experience: West Ham supporter services and marketing, with the Pistons’ fan-experience team.
    • Performance: the two heads of performance and medical.
    • Community: the West Ham United Foundation, with the Pistons’ community team.
  • Term. Three years (April 2027 to March 2030), with a break clause after the year-one evaluation, a conduct and reputation clause, and a data-protection agreement.

6.2 Resources

No rights fee is paid. Each club funds its own staff time and travel. We estimate a joint project budget of about £300,000 per year, shared 50/50, covering documentary production, fan events, the youth exchange and impact measurement. This figure is indicative and would be confirmed in negotiation. The main resource is staff capacity: one partnership manager per club, plus roughly 10% of each pillar lead’s time.

8Critical reflection and limitations

  • Evidence and interpretation. Club histories, results and league developments come from the sources listed below. The strategic needs, selection scores, budget and KPI targets are our own interpretation and estimates.
  • Data limitations. We had no access to internal data on injuries, fan surveys or finances. Baselines must be measured before the targets are confirmed.
  • Alternative view. A football partner such as Schalke 04 would bring more direct sporting benefits, such as player pathways. We judge that the knowledge and fan-experience value of a complementary partner is greater and carries less risk of competition. A football partnership could be added later as a separate initiative.
  • Limits of the partnership. The partnership will not win promotion on its own. It supports the rebuild by strengthening fan engagement, organisational learning and community impact.

9Conclusion

West Ham needs to keep its supporters close, rebuild quickly and stay visible beyond England while it plays in the Championship. The Detroit Pistons have just done what West Ham must now do, they share its industrial, working-class DNA, and they need exactly what West Ham can offer: a credible home in London and Europe. Their resources are complementary, they do not compete, and the story the two clubs can tell together is unique.

“A partnership is not valuable because two organisations look good together. It’s valuable when collaboration creates something strategically useful that neither could create alone.”
