Sunk costs at West Ham United · Area 2 of 3

Head coach: backing the coach, or backing the money?

Four head coaches have been appointed since May 2024. Every change leaves sunk costs behind, and with them the temptation to defend the last decision instead of making the next one.

Sunk cost Money already spent that cannot be recovered, whatever we decide next. Only future costs and benefits should drive a decision.

1What is sunk

Pay-offs to departing coachesLopetegui ~£3–5m, Potter ~£5m (reported estimates). Paid whether the next coach succeeds or not.
Coach-specific signings~£130m in summer 2024 for Lopetegui’s style, then another rebuild in 2025 under Potter.
Backroom staff and search costsCompensation for assistants (~£1m per change, estimated), plus agent and legal fees for each appointment.
Time and know-howPre-seasons, tactical work and staff relationships that leave with each coach.

3Where the bias shows up

DecisionSunk-cost thinkingThe forward-looking question
Keeping a coach“We paid to get him and spent £100m+ on his players. We have to give him time.”→On underlying performance, is he the best option for the rest of the season?
The board’s reputation“Sacking him now admits we got the appointment wrong.”→What will the next six months cost us with him, and what without him?
New coach, old squad“Not my signing. He doesn’t fit.”→Who fits the club’s model from now on, whoever signed him?
A new coach who ignores earlier fees is healthy for team selection. It becomes expensive when every coach demands a new squad and the last coach’s signings become stranded assets.

4What the research says

Staw (1976): decision-makers who are personally responsible for a failing investment put more money into it (escalation of commitment).
Staw, Barsade & Koput (1997): banks wrote off bad loans faster once the executives responsible had left. New decision-makers de-escalate.
Arkes & Blumer (1985): 85% would spend the last 10% of a budget to finish the radar-invisible plane, even though a rival’s plane was better.
Lecture: a coach’s effect on performance is positive in the first years in office, then turns negative. League experience helps.
Decision point now
Scott Parker took over in November 2026 on a 2½-year deal, with West Ham 8th in the Championship. He has won promotion from the Championship before (Fulham, Bournemouth). He should start with a squad review based on future fit, not on who signed whom or for how much.

He should be judged on forward-looking KPIs agreed now, so that neither his contract nor Nuno’s pay-off becomes the reason for the club’s next decision.

5Recommendations

1

A playing model owned by the club

The board and director of football own one playing identity and player profile. Coaches are hired to fit it, so signings stay useful after a change.

Board + DoF · before the Jan 2027 window
2

Agree the review triggers on day one

Forward-looking KPIs (xG difference, points per game against budget rank, player development) are checked every 10 matches. Money already paid is never a reason to keep or to sack.

Board · at every appointment
3

Separate hiring from reviewing

The review panel includes a non-executive director and a data analyst who were not part of the appointment. Every hire starts with a pre-mortem: “What would make us change course in six months?”

Chair · ongoing
4

Contracts that limit what is sunk

Use shorter terms, break clauses and bonuses tied to promotion. Nuno’s deal reportedly meant no compensation if the club was relegated.

Board + legal · every contract
Don’t let yesterday’s investment determine tomorrow’s decision.
