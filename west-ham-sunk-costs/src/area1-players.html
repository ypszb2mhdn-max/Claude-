Sunk costs at West Ham United · Area 1 of 3

Players: when the price tag picks the team

A transfer fee is gone the day it is paid. It should never decide who plays, who stays, or what price we accept when we sell.

Sunk cost Money already spent that cannot be recovered, whatever we decide next. Only future costs and benefits should drive a decision.

1What is sunk

Transfer feesCommitted at signing, even when paid in instalments. Haller £45m (2019), Paquetá £51m (2022), Castellanos ~€29m (2026).
Agent and intermediary feesPaid on completion and never recovered, however the player performs.
Signing-on and loyalty bonusesGuaranteed money, paid whatever the player’s form.
Scouting, medicals and relocationThe cost of finding, checking and settling a new player.
Not sunk: future wages. A sale, loan or release can still avoid them, so they belong in every selection and sale decision.

2How it built up

2019Sébastien Haller joins for a club-record £45m.
Jan 2021Haller sold to Ajax for ~£20m. The club accepts the loss after 18 months.
2022Lucas Paquetá becomes the new club record at £51m.
2024Summer rebuild for Lopetegui: Kilman, Füllkrug, Summerville, Guilherme, Wan-Bissaka (~£130m reported).
Jan 2026Taty Castellanos signs from Lazio for ~€29m on a 4½-year deal during a relegation fight. Paquetá is sold to Flamengo for ~£36.5m.
May–Aug 2026Relegation, then £140–200m+ of sales (Fernandes ~£85m, Summerville ~£55–60m, Diouf up to £40m). Castellanos asks to leave, and the club reportedly won’t sell “on the cheap”.

3Where the bias shows up

DecisionSunk-cost thinkingThe forward-looking question
Team selection“He cost €29m, he has to start.”→Who gives us the best chance of winning the next match?
Selling“We can’t sell him for less than we paid.”→Is his value to us from now on higher than today’s market price plus the wages we save?
Doubling down“Sign players around him so the investment finally pays off.”→Would we make this signing if the earlier one had never happened?

Once a deal is done, the loss and the profit are both history. A sale is good when the price beats the player’s future value to us, not when it beats his old price tag.

4What the research says

Staw & Hoang (1995), NBA: higher draft picks got more minutes and stayed longer, even after controlling for performance.
Keefer (2017), NFL: a higher salary-cap value means more starts, even after controlling for performance.
Hackinger (2019), Bundesliga: higher transfer fees mean more playing time. The effect is small and fades the longer a player stays.
Özaydın (2022): found sunk-cost bias among Premier League coaches but not among Bundesliga coaches.
Leeds, Leeds & Motomura (2015), NBA: no effect found. The evidence is mixed, but none of it shows that playing the expensive player pays off.

5Recommendations

1

Pick the team without the price tags

Selection meetings use a performance dashboard (minutes, xG/xA, duels, physical data) that leaves out fees and wages.

Head coach + analysts · every matchday
2

Zero-based squad review

Before every window, ask of each player: “Would we sign him today, at today’s price and wage?” If not, sell or loan him.

Director of football · each window
3

Set asking prices from the market

Asking prices come from independent valuations and data, never from the purchase price. Walk-away prices are set before talks begin.

Recruitment committee incl. finance
4

Sink less money up front

Use loans with an option to buy, appearance-based add-ons, staggered fees and relegation wage clauses, so less is lost when a signing fails.

Board + legal · every deal
Don’t let yesterday’s investment determine tomorrow’s decision.
