Sunk costs at West Ham United · Area 3 of 3

Stadium and seats: a home we can’t undo, seats sold but empty

The move to Stratford is the biggest sunk cost in the club’s history. Two decisions now depend on looking forward: whether to buy the London Stadium, and how to fill seats fans have already paid for.

Sunk cost Money already spent that cannot be recovered, whatever we decide next. Only future costs and benefits should drive a decision.

1What is sunk

The Boleyn GroundHome since 1904. The sale was agreed in 2014 and the club left in 2016. There is no way back.
£15m towards the conversionWest Ham’s up-front contribution under the 2013 stadium deal.
Ten years of fit-out and relocationShops, hospitality, fan relations and disputes since 2016.
Public money (not ours)~£750m to build and convert the stadium (£429m + £323m). That is sunk for the taxpayer, and it is not a discount for us.
Fans’ sunk cost: a season ticket is paid before a ball is kicked. From that day the money is sunk for the fan, and that affects whether they turn up.

3Where the bias shows up

DecisionSunk-cost thinkingThe forward-looking question
Buying the stadium“We’ve put £15m and ten years into Stratford, so we have to own it.” “The taxpayer spent £750m, so £50m is a bargain.”→Will future income from owning it (control, naming rights, events, redevelopment) beat future costs (running, upkeep, today’s ~£19–20m yearly losses), compared with renting for £2–5m a year?
Fans’ attendance“I’ve paid for it, so I’ll go.” This pull is strong in August, fades by February, and gets weaker when tickets are cheaper.→How do we keep seats full after the pull of money already paid has faded?
Ticket sales“It’s sold out. Job done.”→How many paid-for seats are actually filled, and what does an empty one cost in atmosphere, catering and merchandise?

4What the research says

Arkes & Blumer (1985): theatre season-ticket buyers who paid full price went to more plays than those who got a discount. Money already paid drives attendance.
Gourville & Soman (1998): the pull of a payment fades over time. Gym attendance peaks just after members pay and falls until the next payment.
Lecture: clubs that sell 100% of season tickets still have empty seats, and unused VIP boxes and business seats hurt the atmosphere too.

5Recommendations

1

A forward-looking stadium business case

An independent valuation and a 20-year cash-flow model under Championship and Premier League scenarios. Past contributions and public spending are left out, and a walk-away price is set before talks.

Board + external advisers · before any approach to City Hall
2

Season tickets paid monthly

Interest-free monthly instalments keep the payment “fresh”, so attendance stays steadier through the season.

Ticketing · 2027/28 renewals
3

Use it, share it or lose it

An easy official ticket exchange for every seat. Holders who miss more than a set share of games without releasing their seat lose renewal priority.

Ticketing + supporters’ board
4

Count fans in seats, not tickets sold

Turnstile occupancy becomes the KPI for season tickets, boxes and business seats. Low-demand Championship games get dynamic pricing.

Commercial team · monthly
Don’t let yesterday’s investment determine tomorrow’s decision.
